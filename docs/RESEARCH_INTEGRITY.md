# Research Integrity

1. Evidence comes before conclusions.
2. The project is an external, observational, single-account audit.
3. Results apply to the studied account and period unless broader evidence is obtained.
4. Observational associations are not causal effects.
5. Statistical mixture components are descriptive regimes, not proprietary platform stages.
6. Feed impressions and detailed analytics remain separate measurements.
7. Missing values remain null and are never silently converted to zero.
8. Exploratory and confirmatory analyses remain clearly separated.
9. Raw p-values, adjusted p-values, and Benjamini-Hochberg q-values are reported using correct terminology.
10. Multiple-testing correction must remain visible.
11. Contradictory and null results must not be removed because they weaken a preferred narrative.
12. No platform-suppression conclusion may be predetermined.
13. No platform-clearance conclusion may be manufactured from uncertainty.
14. Automated racial-visibility labels remain preliminary until human validation is complete.
15. Human validation creates a new result set and new release. It must not overwrite the preliminary result set.
16. Published releases are frozen checkpoints.
17. Corrections require a changelog entry and a new version.
18. Previously published artifacts must not be silently replaced.
19. Changes to data coverage, labels, model specifications, or reported denominators require explicit documentation.
20. Every public claim must map to evidence, limitations, and permitted wording.
21. Private source data and public release data must remain separated.
22. Privacy protection takes priority over maximal reproducibility.
23. Lack of public inputs must be disclosed honestly.
24. The repository must never claim that analyses are reproducible when the necessary inputs are withheld.
25. Research updates must preserve the exact historical record of prior releases.
